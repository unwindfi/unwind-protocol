# unwind 101 for non-crypto audiences

## Overview

A perpetual future is an agreement to take on the price exposure of an asset
without owning it, and without an expiry date. If you open a long position on
a stock at 100 and the price moves to 110, you gain as though you had held the
stock, though at no point did you hold it. A short position gains when the
price falls.

Because the position never expires, it needs a mechanism that keeps the two
sides roughly balanced over time. That mechanism is funding, a small payment
that flows between the two sides periodically.

## What makes unwind different

Most venues match orders continuously. An order arrives, and it is matched
against whatever is resting on the book at that instant. This makes the
timing of an order valuable, and produces an industry of participants
competing to be a few milliseconds earlier than each other.

unwind does not match continuously. Orders submitted within a five second
window are collected together, and at the end of the window everyone buying
settles at one price and everyone selling at another. Being earlier within
that window achieves nothing,
because the window has no internal ordering.

The second difference concerns which assets can be traded at all. On most
venues, a market exists because the venue decided to create it. On unwind,
anyone can open a market against any asset whose price can be read publicly
on the Solana blockchain, and no approval is involved at any stage.

## Where the price comes from

The price is not set by unwind and is not produced by a formula over
deposits. It comes from makers, who post the prices they will buy and sell at,
meeting takers, who come to trade against them.

Each window runs two auctions: takers buying against makers selling, and
takers selling against makers buying. In each one, if more people want to buy
than sell at a candidate price, that price is too low, and if the reverse is
true it is too high. The clearing price is the one that trades the greatest
volume. See [Clearing](auction.md).

## What the risks are

Perpetual futures are leveraged instruments. A position can lose more quickly
than a holding of the underlying asset, and a position whose collateral falls
below a threshold is closed automatically, which is called liquidation.

The program that runs this venue has not been audited. Software that holds
collateral can contain errors, and an unaudited program should be treated as
carrying that risk until it has been reviewed.
